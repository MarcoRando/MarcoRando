## About

I am a Postdoctoral Researcher with a joint affiliation with Inria Sophia Antipolis, in the McTAO team, and Université Côte d’Azur, in the Laboratoire J.A. Dieudonné (LJAD). I am supervised by Samuel Vaiter and Jean-Baptiste Caillau.

I received my Ph.D. in Computer Science from the University of Genoa, where I was supervised by Lorenzo Rosasco and Silvia Villa.

My research focuses on **black-box and zeroth-order optimization**, namely the design, implementation, and theoretical analysis of optimization algorithms in settings where first-order information (gradients) is not available. I study these methods across a broad range of settings, including **deterministic and stochastic environments, smooth and non-smooth objectives, convex and non-convex problems, compositional functions, and bilevel optimization**. In particular, I am interested in **finite-difference methods and structured sampling strategies**, including the use of orthogonal and other structured directions to improve gradient estimation and optimization efficiency. I also investigate **variance-reduction techniques for zeroth-order methods**, as well as **zeroth-order bilevel optimization** and **Langevin-based zeroth-order algorithms** for non-convex optimization, with a focus on their convergence properties and complexity. More broadly, my research aims to develop and analyze zeroth-order methods that are computationally efficient in large-scale and stochastic settings.

A related direction of my research concerns **optimization over probability distributions**, where the optimization variables are the parameters defining a probability distribution. This perspective is particularly relevant to **large language models (LLMs)**, whose autoregressive probabilistic structure naturally leads to optimization problems over parametrized distributions. Such a framework encompasses different stages and applications of LLMs, including **pre-training, fine-tuning, and adversarial attacks**. In this context, I am particularly interested in developing **memory-efficient optimization methods for pre-training and fine-tuning**, with a focus on zeroth-order approaches that avoid the need to store or compute full gradients. I also investigate **fast first-order methods for reinforcement-learning-based optimization**, with the goal of efficiently estimating **gradients** while reducing the computational cost associated with sampling. These methods are particularly relevant to **RLHF-based fine-tuning and adversarial attacks for language models**, where the sampling cost can represent a significant computational bottleneck.

I am also interested in the application of **machine learning and optimization to scientific and engineering problems** across a range of domains, including **high-energy physics, robotics, biology, large-scale machine learning, and adversarial machine learning**. In particular, I investigate **reinforcement learning for olfactory navigation in turbulent environments**, where an agent must locate an odor source from sparse and intermittent sensory observations. I have also worked on **gradient-free optimization for robotics**, investigating the automatic tuning of gains in walking-control architectures for humanoid robots. I have also developed optimization-based techniques for **adversarial machine learning** to assess the robustness and vulnerabilities of machine learning models, including applications to **malware detection and large language models**. More recently, I have worked on **scalable kernel methods for large-scale machine learning**, developing memory-efficient and highly parallelizable optimization techniques for kernel ridge regression.
